Background

Why measure colour rather than look at it

Human vision discounts the light it is under: look at a warm photograph for half a minute and the warmth stops registering as warmth. The picture then goes to a phone, a friend's laptop or a print, and the cast is still in the file.

A scope does not adapt. It reports where the colour is, in the same terms today and tomorrow. This is why grading suites have had scopes built in for decades; most photo editors stop at a histogram.

Before downloading

What you would be running

Downloading an executable is a question of trust. These are the things that can be checked.

  • The source is public. Every line, every release, and the full history — on GitHub under the GNU GPL v3 or later.
  • The builds come from that source. Releases are built by GitHub Actions from a tagged commit, and the downloads are served by GitHub, not by this site.
  • It asks for one permission and no more. macOS needs Screen Recording, because reading the screen is the entire job. There is no network access, no account, no telemetry and no auto-updater.
  • Nothing leaves your machine. The pixels it measures are never sent anywhere, because it has nowhere to send them.

Project status

SideScopes is before version 1.0, developed in the open by one person who uses it daily. macOS and Windows have native builds; the Linux port is in progress.

Preferences may change shape between releases without a migration, so an update can return settings to their defaults. Nothing else is destructive — it only ever reads.
